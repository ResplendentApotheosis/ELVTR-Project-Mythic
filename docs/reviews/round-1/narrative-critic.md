# Narrative Critic — Round 1

Scope: story logic, character motivation, tone and theme in `gdd.txt`. Systems balance, tech scope and business are left to other reviewers.

## 1. The level-25 "big reveal" contradicts the Monkey's own open identity question — BLOCKING

**Problem.** The MVP's central story beat can't be written as it stands. At level 25 the Proboscis Monkey announces that "the Nian defeated all 12 guardians; their bodies are stone, their souls scattered" (Story beats, level 25, "Poster (big reveal)"). But Open questions asks: "Is the Proboscis Monkey the zodiac Monkey guardian (the one who escaped), or a separate temple guardian?" If he is the Monkey guardian who escaped, his reveal line is false: not all 12 fell, and the speaker is one of them. That would be a twist the document never sets up or pays off. If he is a separate guardian, then "Guardian of the Jade Temple" (Cast table) is a 13th guardian, and nothing explains why he survived when the 12 did not.

**Passages.** Cast table ("Guardian of the Jade Temple; guide from level 25"). Story beats, level 25. Open questions, bullet 2. The Monkey's arrival is also inconsistent: the Cast table says "guide from level 25", but the beats table says "30 — Monkey joins as guide". The level-25 booster "Monkey's Staff (story gift)" also implies he is already helping at 25.

**Why it matters.** This is the poster beat that unlocks the whole soul-fragment loop and the Zodiac-world premise. Answer the question before the reveal is scripted, and pick one level for when the Monkey joins.

## 2. Level 10's "first freed creature" can't happen: the rescue mechanic debuts at 17 — MAJOR

**Problem.** The story promises a rescue before the game can deliver one. The level-10 beat is "First freed creature tells of a dark beast at the river". But the mummified creature (OB07 "container release", "the core rescue goal") doesn't debut until level 17 in the obstacle table. Level 10 actually debuts the Lantern shrine (OB05). No earlier obstacle frees a creature.

**Related pillar break.** Pillar 2 says "Every board is a rescue", and the Cast table says freeing creatures is "the most common level goal". Yet levels 1–16 have no creatures to free: they are brambles, dew, moss, lanterns and pillars. The intro comic sets up "friends are being wrapped by creep", but the player spends 16 levels never unwrapping anyone. The story's opening hook and the play diverge for the first quarter of the MVP.

**Fix direction.** Either move a simplified creature release (or a scripted, non-obstacle rescue) earlier, or rewrite the level-10 beat so it doesn't depend on a freed creature.

## 3. "A story beat on every hard level" isn't met, and the villain's forces have almost no faces — MAJOR

**Problem.** Pillar 4 promises "A story beat on every hard level (every 5th from 15)". Level progression repeats it: "each hard level carries a mini story beat". But the Story beats table has nothing for hard levels 15, 35, 45 or 55. Of the hard levels in 1–60, only 20, 30 and 40 (plus finales 25 and 50) have beats.

The Cast table says "Nian's forces — Faces of the hard levels, shown in story cards". Only two are named: the Creep Wraith at 20 and the Stone Golem at 40. The asset list budgets only "the Nian and 2 of its forces". So the antagonist side, which is meant to give hard levels their dramatic stakes, has two appearances in 60 levels.

The Nian itself is "never fought directly in the MVP". The only villain pressure the player feels comes from these forces, so the gaps leave long stretches (41–49, 51–60) with no antagonist presence. Meanwhile Nian's Eye (46) and Terracotta minion (56) arrive as mechanics without story framing.

**Smaller structural inconsistency.** Chapters are 25 levels with "a new backdrop every chapter". But Art direction says backgrounds are "One per 10-level chapter", and the asset list budgets "6 backgrounds × 2 states". The document doesn't agree on what a narrative "chapter" is.

## 4. The protagonists have no identity, motivation or arc, and the quest hand-off after the reveal is unmotivated — MAJOR

**Problem.** The four friends are the player's party and "narrate levels and posters". They also deliver a "protagonist quip" on every combo callout. Yet the document gives them no names, personalities, catchphrases or colours: "Names, bios and designs from you", listed in Open questions. With no characters defined, nothing in Story & world can carry motivation, voice or tone. Who speaks the level-10 dialogue, the 30 dialogue, or the 51–60 dialogue is undefined.

**Motivation gap.** The setup is "Four friends set out for the Jade Temple to ask the 12 Zodiac guardians for help". At 25 they learn the guardians have fallen. Nothing in the beats explains why four ordinary friends then take on a 12-guardian, 200+ level restoration quest themselves, or what personal stake keeps them going. The plan was to find help; the help is gone. That pivot needs a beat: a choice, a cost or a promise.

**Ending gap.** The MVP is meant to end on "Dialogue; cliffhanger at 60". Yet the cliffhanger's content is never stated. The Soul fragments schedule puts the Ox's restoration at about level 110, so the MVP ends mid-trail. An undefined cliffhanger is the last thing players see before the retention test the MVP exists to run.

## 5. The fire/noise folklore is inverted by the obstacle themes — MINOR

**Problem.** The theme statement is clear: "because the Nian fears red, fire and noise, the player's best tools are themed that way: firecracker specials, red lanterns that burn away creep". But in the obstacle table the Nian's own hazards, Nian's Eye (OB09) and Nian's Jar (OB13), are both in the Fire element family. They use the "crackle-and-snap" Fire sound palette, the same sonic language as the player's weapons.

Fire is also both the cure and the curse among the obstacles:

- Lantern shrines and Paper-screen lanterns are Fire obstacles the player must *light*. Lighting fits the theme.
- Fire is also the element of the beast's hazards. That doesn't fit.

The document also promises that elements "can later tie to the guardians' elements". So this muddle will spread into Part 2 unless it's fixed now. Consider moving the Nian's hazards into a separate "curse" family, or into Earth or Water (stone and creep already read as the Nian's corruption). Keep Fire, red and noise as the player's side only.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

I read all five colleague reviews: systems-designer, player-psychologist, feasibility-lead, adversarial-qa and business-analyst.

### Conflicts

**C1. Feasibility Lead #1 (cut list includes "the posters") vs my #1 and #4.** I disagree with cutting posters as a block. Two of the three posters are the only delivery vehicle for the MVP's two payoffs: the level-25 reveal ("Poster (big reveal); soul fragments unlock") and the level-50 restoration ("Poster + celebration"). Without them, the story never gives an emotional peak. The pillar is literally "Story as reward". Cut the **level-20 poster** instead. As Business Analyst #5 noted, the story table already delivers 20 as a "story card", so that poster is redundant asset spend. I agree that OB09 and the Crane-carry combo are cut candidates. Cutting OB09 also helps my #5 (see Connection N3).

**C2. Business Analyst #2 (demote "Souls carry forward" to a post-MVP note) vs the story spine.** I agree with the critique of the cross-game parts: Soul Dust, the shared profile, and random off-trail fragments in 51–60. But demoting the pillar outright removes the only thing that turns "four friends clear creep" into a quest. Inside the MVP, fragments *are* the plot. The 25 reveal is the moment they unlock, 30 is "the Rat fragment found at 26 begins to glow", 40 is the Golem attacking the Rat statue, and 50 is the restoration. My position: rewrite the pillar as an in-game promise, something like "Souls restore the world: every fragment visibly brings a guardian closer". Keep cross-game carry-over as a backlog note. The pillar should describe the story being shipped, not be deleted with the franchise plumbing.

**C3. Player Psychologist #5 (level 25 already stacks too many pop-ups) vs my #4 (the reveal needs an added "why we take on the quest" beat).** This is a real tension, because my fix adds content to the most crowded moment in the MVP. I concede the motivation beat must not be another modal at 25. The fix that serves both of us comes from my own #1 inconsistency. Settle the Monkey's arrival at **30**, as the beats table already says. Move the "Monkey's Staff (story gift)" from 25 to 30. Put the pivot beat (the friends' choice to take up the quest) into the level-30 dialogue, alongside the glowing fragment. Level 25 then carries only the reveal poster and the fragment unlock. The motivation beat lands as dialogue at a hard level that currently has a beat but no stakes.

**C4. Systems Designer #5 and Adversarial QA #5 rate the level-10 / OB07 problem MINOR, but I hold MAJOR.** Both treat it as a table inconsistency. It is more than that. Pillar 2 ("Every board is a rescue") is unfulfilled for levels 1–16, and those are exactly the levels inside the day-1 window the MVP exists to measure (Business Analyst #3; Player Psychologist #3: "levels 1–25 have little to chase"). The opening comic promises rescue, and a new player spends the whole first session not rescuing anyone. I support Systems Designer's concrete fix of moving OB07 to 7, swapped with moss. That makes the level-10 beat true without adding to the early-level load that Player Psychologist #2 warns about.

### Connections

**N1. The fragment-goal gap (Systems Designer #1, Adversarial QA #1) also breaks the script, not just the level-50 count.** The level-30 beat is "the Rat fragment found at 26 begins to glow". If a player can win level 26 without freeing the cocoon, the level-30 dialogue refers to an object they never picked up. Level 40 ("Golem tries to smash the Rat's statue") and level 50 make the same assumption. StoryPlayer data is static (`data/story/*.json`), so every fragment-referencing line is written as if collection is guaranteed. This raises the stakes on their BLOCKING: whichever rule they choose (fragment always a goal, or auto-collected on win), the story needs it to guarantee collection. A "fragment optional" ruling is narratively unshippable.

**N2. The soul-fragment arithmetic quietly answers my Open-question finding #1, and it forces a choice.** The schedule restores Rat, Ox, Rabbit and Dog. It then says "other 8 guardians" each hold about 2 of 12, which makes 12 guardians needing restoration. That only works if all 12 fell, so the economy has already assumed the Monkey is *not* the zodiac Monkey guardian. If the designer instead picks "the Monkey guardian who escaped", there are 11 fallen guardians. Then the random 30% pool would have to exclude the Monkey, and the "8 others" math, which Systems Designer #4 and Adversarial QA #2 already show is broken, changes again. Recommendation: make the Monkey a separate temple guardian, and give one line at 25 or 30 for why he survived (for example, he was away from the temple, or the temple's lanterns shielded him, which would tie to the fire theme). This fixes the logic for both the story and the fragment table at once.

**N3. Nian's Eye at 46 (Player Psychologist #4) is also the villain's first on-board appearance, and it arrives with no story.** Player Psychologist argues the most-hated hazard lands at the worst moment, right before the Rat climax. My #3 noted 41–49 has no antagonist presence, and my #5 noted the Eye is wrongly Fire-themed. Combined: moving OB09's debut into 51–56, as Player Psychologist suggests, is better for *story* too. After the Rat is restored, the Nian *notices*: its Eye opens on the board. That gives the Ox chapter its antagonist escalation and gives the undefined level-60 cliffhanger (my #4) something to escalate toward. Re-element it as "curse", not Fire, at the same time. Three reviewers' fixes converge on one change.

**N4. Story payoffs sit exactly on the difficulty walls (Systems Designer #3, Player Psychologist #1).** Both show roughly 1 in 8 to 1 in 4 players can run out of lives on finales 25 and 50. Those are the two posters, the reveal and the restoration. The "Story as reward" pillar is designed so the reward is behind the hardest gate in each chapter, then gated again by a lives timer. Narratively, a reveal that a fifth of players reach after a 100-minute wait loses its momentum. I back Systems Designer's suggestion that story finales should not be the hardest boards in their chapters. As an alternative, deliver the poster on *entry* to the finale (the reveal motivates the fight) and the chapter card on the win.

**N5. Guardian powers that run out (Systems Designer #2, Player Psychologist #3) undercut the restoration's meaning.** The level-50 beat is "the Rat wakes and gives the party its blessing (first super power)". Rat's Nibble then has 3 uses and never refills. A guardian's blessing that disappears after three taps tells players that restoring guardians doesn't last, which is the opposite of the game's theme of healing the forest. The Monkey's Staff, "story gift", has 1 use. Story gifts should be renewable (one use per level, or a charge meter, as Systems Designer suggests). This is a narrative argument for Systems Designer #2's fix, not just an economy one.

**N6. "Story-complete Act 1" (Business Analyst #1, Feasibility Lead #1) contradicts the narrative's own ending.** Both flag the sentence as undefined scope creep. From the story side, the document defines no acts, and the MVP's planned ending is "cliffhanger at 60", which is the opposite of story-complete. The only complete arc in the document is 1–50: journey, reveal, trail, restoration. If "Act 1" means anything, the story structure points to it ending at 50, which would *shrink* scope, not grow it. Either way the designer must pick one: an MVP that ends on a complete arc at 50, or one that ends on a cliffhanger at 60. The document currently claims both.

**N7. No audience (Business Analyst #3) plus undefined protagonists (my #4) means no tone.** The cast mixes a "comic mentor with a big nose and bigger opinions" with a premise where all 12 guardians are dead and their souls are shattered. Whether that is played as slapstick or folklore melancholy depends on who the game is for, and the document names neither the audience nor the protagonists' voices. Writing the 51–60 dialogue and every combo "protagonist quip" is blocked on both answers.

### Revisions

- **#1: downgrade BLOCKING → MAJOR.** N2 shows the fragment economy already assumes 12 fallen guardians, so the resolution is effectively forced: a separate temple guardian plus one survival line. The Monkey-joins-at-25-or-30 conflict is resolved by C3. It is still a must-answer before the week-14 "temple reveal playable" milestone, but it is a one-decision fix, not a structural flaw.
- **#2: hold at MAJOR** against Systems Designer and Adversarial QA's MINOR (see C4).
- **#3: split.** Upgrade the missing hard-level beats. Level 15 is the first hard level, and it is beatless in the reward-thin 1–25 stretch (Player Psychologist #3). Hard-level beats are the cheapest fix there: a story card per hard level, reusing existing cast art. Withdraw my "chapter" background sub-point to Business Analyst #5 and Adversarial QA #5, who own it in budget and asset terms. I add nothing to their version.
- **#4: upgrade the ending gap within MAJOR.** Combined with N6 and Business Analyst #3 ("prove retention" with no success test), the undefined level-60 cliffhanger is the final impression before the D1/D7 measurement. N3 gives it concrete content: the Nian's Eye opens and the Ox trail begins.
- **#5: correct the scope, keep MINOR.** Nian's Jar (OB13) debuts at 86, outside the MVP, so within 1–60 only Nian's Eye carries the fire inversion. The fix is cheap if folded into N3's re-timing of OB09.

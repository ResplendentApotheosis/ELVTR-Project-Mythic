# Systems Designer — Round 1

Lane: core loop, progression, pacing, difficulty curve, economy math. Five findings, most severe first.

## 1. The rules never say whether a soul fragment is a goal, and the MVP's main payoff depends on that — BLOCKING

**Problem.** The MVP builds up to "Rat restored at exactly level 50". That only works if the player collects the fragment on every scripted fragment level (26, 30, 33, 35, 38, 40, 45, 50). The document never says how a fragment relates to the win condition. Both possible readings break something else:
- **If the fragment is not a goal**, a player can clear every listed goal, win level 33 and leave the cocoon closed. The rule "Fail the level and the fragment stays for the retry" only covers losing. Nothing covers a win where the fragment was not collected, so the "exactly level 50" promise can fail. Level 50 is then a finale that may not restore the Rat.
- **If the fragment is a goal**, it adds one to the goal count. Six of the eight Rat fragment levels are hard or finale levels (30, 35, 40, 45 hard; 50 finale), so their counts go above the caps: hard levels are capped at "3–4 goals" and finales at "4 goals". The finale would have 5 goals, which is the goal load the Research section blames for MV's stalls. The level bot's win targets would then be tuned on boards with one more goal than the tier tables assume.

**Source.** In Soul fragments: "The Rat's 8 fragments are scripted so it is restored at exactly level 50", "Fail the level and the fragment stays for the retry", and "Hard and finale levels always carry a fragment". In Core gameplay: "Win: all goals done before moves run out" and "3–4 on hard levels, 4 on finales". The level JSON adds `"fragment": {"guardian": "rat", "scripted": true}` but has no goal semantics.

**Fix direction.** Choose one rule and write it down. Either the fragment is always one of the tier's goals (it uses one of the 3–4 slots, not an extra), or the fragment is auto-collected on any win. Make the level bot and the validation checklist enforce whichever rule you choose.

## 2. Guardian powers and boosters run out after their free uses, and coins have only one use — MAJOR

**Problem.** The Meta & economy section calls guardian powers "the big rewards", but every booster, including the first guardian power, is a fixed number of free uses that never refills. Rat's Nibble, the payoff for the whole Chapter 2 arc, is 3 uses and then gone. No coin price is listed for any booster, and the only stated coin spend is the 300-coin continue. A single booster bought later would also need either a coin price, which conflicts with "no pay-to-pass", or a refill source. Neither exists. The result: after about level 30 the booster bar is empty, and coins pile up with one use. The unlock schedule (11/16/22/25/29/50) becomes a set of tutorials for tools the player won't have later, so they don't connect to the rest of the loop. The win-streak chest gives free specials on the board, not booster stock, so it doesn't fill the gap.

**Source.** In Meta & economy, the Booster unlocks table's "Free uses" column (3 / 1 each / 1 / 1 / 1 / 3), "Coins … Win = 20 + 5 per finale rocket", "Continue … 300 coins". The document never says how boosters are bought or refilled. A rough estimate: about 60 wins × about 40 coins, plus the 500 starting coins, is about 2,900 coins by level 60, and the continue is the only place to spend them.

**Fix direction.** Make guardian powers renew, for example a charge meter filled by matches or one free use per level, so the "big reward" stays in play. Give coins at least one booster price or refill path that stays fair. Then the level bot needs to know whether players are expected to have boosters on hard levels.

## 3. The difficulty curve rests on a bot-to-human mapping the document never defines, and its steepest points land on the story payoffs — MAJOR

**Problem.**
- **The calibration is undefined.** Every tier target (95% / 85–90% / 60–70% / 35–45% / 25–35%) is a human first-try win rate. The only tool that measures it is a headless bot, which plays 500 times and reports the bot's own win rate. The document never says how strong the bot is, whether it plays like a human (greedy, lookahead, special-seeking), or whether it uses boosters or the continue. Bot and human win rates can differ a lot, so the curve as tuned is unknown. The fairness rule "tuned … to their win targets, never above" is also ambiguous. It can mean "never harder than the target" or "the win rate never above the target", and those are opposite constraints.
- **The walls fall on the payoff levels.** Level 25 (temple reveal) and level 50 (Rat restored) are finales with a 25–35% first-try target. With 5 lives and a 1-per-20-minute refill, a player at 25% fails all five tries 0.75^5 ≈ 24% of the time, and at 35% it's about 12%. So roughly 1 in 8 to 1 in 4 players get locked out of lives on the two biggest story moments. The relief valve, "30 min of unlimited lives", comes after the finale, when the player no longer needs it.

**Source.** In Level progression, the Level tiers table. In Technical design: "Headless bot: plays each level 500× and reports first-try win rate" and "if the win rate misses the tier target, adjust goal counts (±20%) or moves (±2)". In Meta & economy, the Lives and Fairness rules.

**Fix direction.** Set a bot-to-human offset from the week-9 grey-box testers before tuning levels 1–60. State plainly whether the targets include boosters and continues. Either move the unlimited-lives window to start on finale entry, or make story finales a little easier than mechanical finales. The 25 and 50 boards don't have to be the hardest boards in their chapters.

## 4. Fragment pacing drops by about half right after the Rat, and the schedule table doesn't match its own rules — MAJOR

**Problem.**
- **The cadence slows after the MVP's first big reward.** The Rat takes 8 fragments over 25 levels, about one restoration per 25 levels. After that each guardian needs 12 fragments, and the document plans about 50–60 levels per guardian. The MVP ends at 60 with the Ox at about 3 of 12 fragments, so the cliffhanger is followed by roughly 40–50 levels before the next restoration.
- **The schedule doesn't follow from the stated rules.** "Every hard level and about 1 in 5 other levels" works out to about 1.8 drops per 5 levels (≈0.36 per level), so about 0.25 trail fragments per level. At that rate the Ox finishes around level 100, not "around 110", and Rabbit and Dog arrive earlier than the table says too.
- **The random-drop arithmetic double counts.** The 30% random share is spread over every non-trail guardian. That pool includes Ox, Rabbit and Dog before their trails, and it may include the restored Rat, whose fragments turn into Soul Dust. The document doesn't say. About 17 random drops spread over 10–11 guardians gives about 1.5 each, not "about 2 of 12" for the other 8. Dog cannot "already hold ~3" from random drops between 51 and 165, since that window has only about 11 random drops across 10–11 guardians, so about 1 for Dog. With roughly 1 to start and about 8–9 trail fragments from 166–200, the Dog is not restored by 200.

Soul fragments are the long-term progression spine and the cross-game currency (the "Souls carry forward" design pillar), so an inconsistent baseline here carries through the whole Zodiac world.

**Source.** In Soul fragments, the rules, the Schedule table (the "Dog (already holds ~3 from random drops)" row) and the "Arithmetic check" paragraph. The document doesn't say whether the random pool excludes restored guardians.

**Fix direction.** Redo the schedule in a small spreadsheet driven by the actual rules. State the random pool explicitly, with restored guardians excluded. Consider 8–10 fragments for the next 2–3 guardians so the slowdown after the Rat is a ramp rather than a cliff.

## 5. The obstacle debut table breaks the "debut after a hard level" rule, and the core rescue goal arrives after its own story beat — MINOR

**Problem.**
- **Debut timing.** The document states three times that new obstacles debut on the relief level right after a hard level. The debut table doesn't follow this through level 34. Hard levels are 15, 20, 25 and 30, so relief levels are 16, 21, 26 and 31, but the debuts are at 17, 22, 28 and 34. Only 41, 46 and 56 onward match. Since the level agent is handed "one 5-level block of the plan at a time" along with the brief's rules, it gets two conflicting instructions on 4 of the 12 MVP debuts.
- **Rescue goal timing.** "Mummified creature" (OB07) is described as "the core rescue goal", and the design pillar "Every board is a rescue" calls freeing creatures "the most common level goal". It doesn't debut until level 17. Yet the level-10 story beat is "First freed creature tells of a dark beast". The first 16 levels cannot contain the rescue mechanic the pillar and the story promise.

**Source.** The Board layers & obstacles debut table and its rule "New obstacles debut on the relief level right after a hard level". In Level progression: "a relief level after each hard one where new obstacles debut". The Research basis table: "debuting on the level after a hard one". The Story beats row for level 10, and the Design pillars.

**Fix direction.** Either shift the early debuts to 16/21/26/31, or state that the rule applies only from level 41. Move OB07 to an early slot (for example 7 or 10, swapped with moss or lantern) so the level-10 beat and the rescue pillar are true from the first chapter.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

I read all five colleague reviews. Below: where I disagree, what only shows up when their findings are combined with mine, and how my own calls change.

### CONFLICTS

**C1. Player Psychologist #1 ("the continue is almost never affordable") vs. my #2 ("coins pile up with one use").** These look opposite, and both of us were partly wrong. My ~2,900-coin figure assumes a player who never continues. The Psychologist's "5–8 more wins" between continues is right for a player who does. The point that settles it is in the coin formula: "Win = 20 + 5 per finale rocket", where rockets come from leftover moves. Hard and finale boards are tuned to 25–45% win rates, so wins there end with few or no leftover moves and pay close to the flat 20 coins. **Coin income is lowest on exactly the levels where the continue is needed.** So the coin balance swings both ways. A player who coasts through regular levels builds up a pile they can't spend. A player stuck on a hard level has just earned the least. My side of the argument: the Psychologist's fix ("bring the continue cost in line with coin income") is not enough on its own. If boosters get a coin price, which both of us recommend, coins are competed for and affordability gets worse again. The document needs one coin budget per 5-level block (≈4.5 wins × expected payout, set against continue and booster prices). Tuning the continue price alone, or the booster prices alone, won't fix it.

**C2. Business Analyst #5 ("post-60 content is decorative; don't build the full 70/30 trail with pity") vs. my #4 (redo the fragment schedule).** The Analyst is right that the 110/165/200 arithmetic isn't needed to ship the MVP, and I'm narrowing my finding below. My side: levels 51–60 are in the MVP, and the GDD ships the 70/30 roll and "first random fragments of other guardians" there. SaveService persists those counts in "one player profile shared by every Zodiac game". Once a fragment count has shipped to players' saves, it can't be quietly rebalanced later. So the random-pool definition (does it include the restored Rat and the future trail guardians?) is an MVP decision even though the later restorations are not. The two positions can be reconciled: do what the Analyst and the Psychologist (#3) both suggest and ship 51–60 as trail-only (Ox) drops. That avoids building the random/pity system and avoids shipping broken numbers into a persistent cross-game save.

**C3. Business Analyst #5 ("the art budget is about 2× too high"; reconcile backgrounds with the 25-level chapter) — and Feasibility #4b, which treats it as a contradiction to resolve either way.** I argue the fix should go the other way. Psychologist #3 shows levels 1–25 have almost no progression signal: no fragments, an undesigned meta, coins with one sink. The Art direction calls the forest's visible shift from decay to colour "the core visual story". With 25-level chapters, that shift happens once in the entire first hour. The 10-level cadence (6 backgrounds) is the only progression feedback that runs through chapter 1. Keep the art count, change the text to "a new backdrop every 10 levels", and treat this as a pacing decision rather than a budget cut.

**C4. Adversarial QA #2 ("a non-trail guardian can be restored out of order").** I disagree that this needs design time now. By my Round 1 math, about 17 random drops across levels 51–200 are spread over 10–11 guardians, roughly 1.5 each. Finishing a 12-fragment guardian from random drops alone before level 200 is close to impossible, and the pity rule pushes drops further toward the trail. QA's other point, the **reroll exploit**, is the real issue in that cluster, and I'd rank it above the out-of-order case. It only exists if the roll happens on entry and the map icon shows which guardian the fragment is for. Under the C2 fix (trail-only drops in the MVP) it disappears for the MVP entirely.

### CONNECTIONS

**X1. Fragment-as-goal (my #1, QA #1) × special targeting (Feasibility #3, QA #4) × Nian's Eye on the Rat finale (Psychologist #4).** The open fragment rule doesn't just decide whether the Rat can be restored. It also decides how the specials behave:
- The Paper Crane "flies to a goal tile". If the fragment is not a goal, the signature special ignores the one item the story is about.
- Jade Orb + Jade Orb "clears the whole board", and a drop item (OB11) is a content-layer piece. It's undefined whether a board clear collects the fragment, destroys it, or skips it.
- The hazard rule protects goals only ("never put a goal behind a hazard's beam"). If the fragment is not a goal, nothing stops it from being placed in the beam's range.

Level 50 combines all of these. It is a finale (4 goals, 25–35%) and carries a mandatory fragment. It falls inside the "3–5 levels after an intro each include it" window for Nian's Eye (46), and it is the Rat restoration. The document hasn't decided whether level 50 might have 5 goals, a beam hazard, and a fragment the player can't reliably target with specials. My #1 fix now needs a second line: **the fragment is a goal for targeting purposes (Crane, hazard placement, board clears) and counts within the tier's goal cap.** Psychologist #4's fix (no hazard on 50, or move OB09 past 50) should be adopted at the same time.

**X2. Bot calibration (my #3, Feasibility #4a) × untaught combos (Psychologist #2).** Feasibility and I both flagged that the bot's play policy is undefined. The Psychologist adds the specific mechanism by which the bot will be wrong. No level teaches any of the six combos, so any bot that seeks out special-on-special swaps is playing a game most humans don't know about. The error then has a known direction: the bot will **overstate** human win rates, and the error grows with goal load, so it is worst on hard and finale levels. Applied to my lockout arithmetic: if a finale tuned to 30% bot win rate plays at about 20% for humans, the chance of losing all 5 lives goes from about 17% to about 33%. That's one in three players stuck at the temple reveal. Practical consequence: the bot needs a "no-combo / greedy-human" mode as its tuning baseline, or combo tutorials have to ship before level 15.

**X3. Retention test (Business Analyst #3) × difficulty walls (my #3, Psychologist #1).** The Analyst points out that the week-20 milestone tracks "day-1 return and level fail rates" with no success threshold. Add my finding: the fail rates on 25 and 50 are set by an uncalibrated bot plus a lives system that locks players out. So the MVP's one experiment would mostly be measuring tuning error. A bad D1 number couldn't tell "players don't want this game" apart from "players hit a 100-minute wall at level 25". The retention bar the Analyst asks for is only interpretable after the bot-to-human offset from the week-9 testers exists.

**X4. Fragment rule contradiction (Feasibility #4b, QA #3) × my #1.** I missed that "Hard and finale levels always carry a fragment" contradicts "1–25: None" (hard 15, 20, finale 25). In my lens this matters more than a wording fix: if the fragment becomes a goal within the cap (X1), the level agent applying the rule literally would put a fragment goal on 15/20/25 with nothing to restore. The rule needs a scope clause ("from level 26").

**X5. Undefined "Act 1" (Business Analyst #1) × my #4 pacing cliff.** If "story-complete Act 1" means the next restoration payoff, the Ox lands around level 100–110 under the stated rules. That nearly doubles the MVP level count. The fragment cadence I flagged is therefore also a scope issue. It's another reason to take the Analyst's position and delete the Act 1 sentence rather than define it.

**X6. Cut list (Feasibility #1).** From a systems view, I endorse cutting OB09 (Nian's Eye) from the MVP. It removes most of QA #4's undefined interactions (Crane path vs. beam, destroyed specials counting as hits), Psychologist #4's climax problem, and X1's level-50 stack in one move. The cost is breaking the research parity of "12 obstacles by 56"; 11 is fine. I'd keep the Crane-carry combo off the cut list only if X1's targeting rule is written. Otherwise cut it too, since it's the combo that depends most on the undefined goal semantics.

### REVISIONS

- **#1 (fragment is a goal?) — BLOCKING, strengthened.** QA reached the same conclusion independently, and X1 widens it from a win-condition gap to a gap in special targeting and hazard placement. The fix now has to cover targeting, the cap, and the "from 26" scope (X4).
- **#2 (boosters/coins) — stays MAJOR, reframed.** I withdraw "coins pile up" as a general claim. It's true only for players who don't continue. The real defect is that coin income runs against difficulty (C1) and there is no coin budget. The point that guardian powers don't renew stands unchanged.
- **#3 (bot calibration and finale walls) — upgraded to BLOCKING.** Three reviewers (Psychologist #1, Feasibility #4a, me) reached it independently. X2 gives the bot error a known direction that makes the walls worse, and X3 shows it undermines the MVP's only stated success measure.
- **#4 (fragment schedule) — split and downgraded.** The post-60 arithmetic (Ox ~100 vs ~110, Dog's ~3, the double count) goes to **MINOR**: the Analyst is right that it's outside the MVP's critical path. The in-MVP part, shipping undefined random drops into a persistent cross-game save in 51–60, stays **MAJOR**, and the fix is trail-only drops in the MVP (C2).
- **#5 (debut timing, rescue at 17) — stays MINOR.** Narrative #2 and QA #5 confirm the level-10 rescue problem. Psychologist #2 adds that level 22 stacks the OB08 debut with the Bamboo Sweep unlock. Small correction to the Psychologist: 28 is three levels after hard level 25, not two, which supports Feasibility's point that 28 and 34 don't fit any reading of the rule.

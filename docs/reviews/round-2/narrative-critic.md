# Narrative Critic — Round 1

Scope: story logic, character motivation, tone, theme, dialogue intent. Systems, scope and business left to other reviewers.

## 1. The level-60 cliffhanger contradicts the 35–45 beats — MAJOR

**Problem.** The MVP's closing hook relies on the Nian only now discovering the heroes' work. But by that point the document has already shown the Nian's forces trying to stop the restoration three times. The "it knows now" turn has no surprise left in it, and the closing line lands on stakes the player has already met.

**Passage.** Level 60: "Cliffhanger: the Nian notices the Rat has woken. Its great Eye opens over the Ox's valley." Compare these earlier beats:
- Level 35: "the Creep Wraith returns to snatch the next Rat fragment"
- Level 40: "Nian's Stone Golem tries to smash the Rat's statue"
- Level 45: "the Stone Golem stirs again"

The Nian's own servants are going after Rat fragments and the Rat's statue, so the Nian already knows about the restoration effort.

**Fix direction.** Either recast 35–45 as the forces reacting blindly to the spreading glow, not knowingly guarding the Rat, or change the 60 beat to an escalation. For example, the Nian stops sending servants and comes itself. That would also give the never-fought villain a reason to appear in person.

## 2. The player collects fragments before the heroes decide to restore anyone — MAJOR

**Problem.** In the story, the decision to restore the guardians comes four levels after the mechanic that carries it out. From levels 26 to 29, the player frees Rat soul fragments with no on-screen reason. The heroes' turning point at 30 ("the four will wake the guardians themselves") then only confirms what the player has already been doing. It should be the motivation that starts it.

**Passage.** The Overview and Story & world say: "At level 30 they choose to restore the guardians themselves." Level 25 is delivered as "Poster (big reveal); soul fragments unlock." The Soul fragments section says: "Fragments start at level 26, right after the temple reveal." Level 30 adds: "The Rat fragment freed at 26 begins to glow." So 26 is a fragment level before any character has said what fragments are for. The Monkey's level-25 reveal only says the souls are "scattered". He never says they can be gathered or put back.

**Fix direction.** Move the decision into the level-25 poster, or end that poster on a clear "the souls can be gathered" line from the Monkey. Then level 30 becomes the first proof that it works, and the glow pays off a promise already made.

## 3. The script breaks its own rules for how the heroes talk — MAJOR

**Problem.** The document makes Kancil the narrative spine and sets a firm speaking pattern. The written beats repeatedly ignore it, starting with the first thing the player sees. This undercuts the "clever trickster leads" premise and the Binturong-asks / Kancil-answers pattern meant to carry exposition.

**Passage.**
- Rule: "Kancil speaks in every story beat, poster and chapter card; the other three never outnumber Kancil's lines."
- Level 1 intro comic: Peafowl, Binturong and Red Panda have 3 lines between them and Kancil has 1, so the very first scene breaks the outnumbering rule.
- Beats with no Kancil line at all: 10 (a freed creature speaks), 35 (Red Panda spots), 40 (no speaker), 45 (Peafowl warns), 50 (the Rat gives its blessing).
- Level 55: "Binturong asks whether the Ox can hear them, and the Rat says yes." Binturong's question is answered by the Rat, not Kancil, which breaks the stated "Binturong asks, Kancil answers with a plan" pattern.
- After 15, Kancil never again "wins by thinking". Levels 35 and 45 are saved by Red Panda's and Peafowl's senses. Nothing in 20–60 is a Kancil trick, so the trickster identity set up at 15 is dropped.

**Fix direction.** Either loosen the rule, or give Kancil the closing line or plan in each beat. Adding a second Kancil trick in chapter 2 (for example at 40 against the Golem) would also pay off the character's premise.

## 4. The antagonists are thinly defined and the folklore logic doesn't hold together — MINOR

**Problem.** The villain side has gaps the story beats depend on.
- **Level 20 mid-boss.** "First mid-boss guards the temple road" has no identity. The Cast lists only "Two forces in the MVP, the Creep Wraith (from 15) and the Stone Golem (from 40)", and the Golem doesn't arrive until 40. So it's unclear who the level-20 boss is, and whether it's simply the Wraith again.
- **Level 40 delivery.** Level 40 is delivered as a "Boss level", but no such format is described anywhere, unlike "Story card" or "Poster".
- **Folklore logic.** The temple's "red lanterns kept the Nian out", and the Nian "fears red, fire and loud noise". Yet all 12 guardians fell. The document never says where or how they fell, or why beings tied to elements, Fire among them, couldn't use the Nian's known weakness when four small animals can. The reveal raises this question and leaves it unanswered.
- **Statue location.** The location of "the Rat's statue" (40, 50) is never given, so chapter 2, titled "Rat's trail", has no sense of journey or place.

**Fix direction.**
- Name the level-20 force, or say it is the Wraith.
- Add one line to the Monkey's reveal on why the guardians fell. For example, the Nian struck before the lanterns were lit, or caught the guardians away from the temple.
- Say where the Rat's statue stands.

## 5. Fixed hero barks name the wrong guardian after level 50 — MINOR

**Problem.** The bark table hard-codes the Rat into Kancil's "Fragment freed" line. From level 51 every fragment is for the Ox, so the lead narrator would keep saying the wrong guardian's name on every Ox fragment. That undermines the "Every fragment visibly brings a guardian closer" promise just as the second guardian's arc begins.

**Passage.** In the bark table, Kancil's "Fragment freed" line is "One more piece of the Rat's soul." The Soul fragments section says: "In the MVP, every fragment from 51–60 is an Ox fragment."

**Fix direction.** Make the line refer to whichever guardian the fragment belongs to ("One more piece of the {guardian}'s soul."), or write a set of lines for each guardian.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

### Business Analyst

**CONFLICT — BA #5 lists the Staff, the Nibble and the quip matrix as "decorative extras". Two of them carry the story.** BA groups "two free-every-level powers (Monkey's Staff, Rat's Nibble)" and "a 4-hero × 7-moment quip matrix" with the win-streak chest and the coin economy as non-load-bearing. Through my lens they are not the same kind of thing.
- **The Rat's Nibble is the only tangible payoff of chapter 2.** The level-50 beat is "the Rat wakes and gives the party its blessing (Rat's Nibble...)". Other than one poster, this is the only place where the pillar "Souls restore the world" changes anything the player can touch. If it goes, 25 levels of fragment collecting end in a picture and no change to play. The coin economy, pre-level boosters and streak chest have no story attached, so I agree with cutting those first. The Nibble should be the last of the gifts to go.
- **The quip matrix is how the "heroes who talk" hook reaches the board.** BA's own fix in #4 says the strongest hook is "the four-hero ... rescue story plus the hero quips". #5 then lists the quip matrix as something to cut. Both can't stand. The matrix is 28 short text lines, and it is the only place the heroes speak between story beats (nothing from 2–9 or 11–14). Cutting it would remove the differentiator BA recommends.

**CONNECTION — BA #4 (heroes are mostly reskins) and my #3 (Kancil's trickster identity is dropped) are the same problem seen from two sides.** BA says the boosters are standard: single tile, 3×3, line, shuffle. I found that Kancil never "wins by thinking" after level 15. Kancil's booster, Hoof Tap, "removes one single tile", which is the plainest tool in the set and has nothing tricky about it. So the lead's personality appears neither in the script nor on the board. If heroes are the "why switch" pitch, Kancil needs at least one moment in each layer, a beat and a mechanic, that only a trickster would do. That makes #3 a positioning problem as well as a writing problem.

**CONNECTION — BA #2 (Lunar New Year hook) relies on the folklore that my #4 found broken.** The LNY positioning depends on the Nian's fear of "red, fire and loud noise", which is also the doc's best thematic thread (lanterns, firecrackers, Fire "only on the player's side"). But the reveal says the lanterns protected the temple while all 12 guardians fell, and it never explains why. For an audience BA identifies as already knowing the Nian legend, this is the gap they are most likely to notice.

### Systems Designer

**CONNECTION — Systems #1 (free charges break calibration) also hits the villain story.** Systems says the Monkey's Staff "is close to an auto-clear on ... multi-cell pillars (OB06)", which are Earth/stone obstacles. Chapter 2's only escalation is the Stone Golem at 40 and 45 ("tries to smash the Rat's statue", "stirs again"). From level 30 the player holds a free, every-level tool that strips the stone obstacles the Golem stands for. So the story card says the threat is growing while the board says it was solved ten levels ago. I support Systems' calibration fix, but the gameplay fix and the story fix have to be designed together. Either the Golem's levels lean on obstacles the Staff doesn't trivialise (frozen idol at 41, goo), or the 40 beat is rewritten as the Golem resisting the Staff.

**CONFLICT (partial) — Systems #1 says "the level-50 finale, the arc's climax, will likely feel flat". Players don't measure a climax only by difficulty.** The doc deliberately makes 25 and 50 "never the hardest board in their chapter". Systems #3 and QA #4 correctly show that the numbers make this rule impossible to meet. But the intent, that a story finale should feel like a celebration rather than a wall, is sound narrative design and should survive the fix. I'd take QA's second option, "define 'hardest' by a measure other than win rate", over raising finales into the hard band. Level 50 feels like a climax because of the payoff at 50 (statue cracks, Rat wakes, power granted). A hard 35% board doesn't add to that.

**CONNECTION — Systems #2 (content runs out before D7) and Systems #5 (Ox may sit at 2/12) make my #1 and #5 worse.** The level-60 cliffhanger is the last thing every finishing player sees, and Systems shows most engaged players will reach it well before day 7. So the MVP's final impression is a reveal that doesn't surprise (my #1), about a guardian the player has barely started (maybe 2/12), voiced by barks that call it the Rat (my #5), and pointing to a level 61 that doesn't exist. Narratively, 51–60 is the weakest stretch in the MVP. That gives a story reason to back the doc's own fallback, "ship 1–50 and end on the Rat's restoration as a complete arc". I don't think that fallback should be held back for a slip. If 51–60 ships, it needs the fixes to #1 and #5 and a scripted Ox count (Systems' "4/12 by level 60").

### Player Psychologist

**CONNECTION — Psych #2 (fragment progress is invisible) upgrades my #5.** Psych shows that "the only feedback for a freed fragment is a hero quip". In Round 1 I treated the wrong-name bark as a small continuity slip. If that bark is the only per-fragment feedback in the game, then from level 51 the only signal of progress tells the player the wrong thing. Psych's proposed meter and my fix belong together, as one "fragment-freed moment" spec covering line, meter and statue stage.

**CONNECTION — Psych #5 (story can't be rewatched) plus my #2 means the explanation of fragments can be lost for good.** Under my #2, the level-25 poster is the only place fragments are explained, and even it only says the souls are "scattered". Psych points out that cards can be tapped through and never seen again. A player who skips the 25 poster then collects fragments from 26 to 29 with no explanation at all, and can never recover it. That makes the "Story so far" screen and moving the decision into level 25 a pair. Either one alone leaves a gap.

**CONFLICT (partial) — Psych #3 says the reason to return in levels 1–25 is "only more levels". The story does provide a goal, but it is only shown four times.** The quest is clear from the first comic ("We need to get to the Zodiac guardians... The Jade Temple is upriver"), and chapter 1 is titled "Journey to the Jade Temple". A goal exists. What's missing is anything that shows it between beats: no beats from 2–9 or 11–14, and no sign of how far upriver the party has come. So I'd frame the fix as "make the existing journey visible" (a map route to the temple) rather than adding a new system. That is cheaper and fits the story. I also concede that my #2 fix (move the decision to 25) does nothing for 1–25, so Psych's finding stands on its own.

**CONNECTION — Psych #4 (rescue waits until level 7) is also a story-to-board break in the opening scene.** The intro comic's first panel is "creep wraps a sleeping hedgehog", and the comic plays after the player's first swap. The game shows a creature to rescue and then delays the rescue goal for six levels. Psych's fix (mummified creature at 1–2, with the hedgehog specifically) also pays off the comic's first image.

### Adversarial QA

**CONNECTION — QA #3 (the level-34 OB11 intro can't legally carry a fragment) has a story-side fix.** The "lost hatchlings" in OB11's name are never mentioned anywhere in the story, yet they are the only valid intro goal at 34. Making level 34 a hatchling rescue fits "Every board is a rescue" better than a fragment would. It also sets up level 35, the first legal drop-item fragment, where "the Creep Wraith returns to snatch the next Rat fragment". A fragment that has to be carried to the bottom row while something tries to snatch it matches the story card well. QA's fix and the 35 beat support each other, and the doc should state that link.

**CONFLICT (minor) — QA #5 rates the free charges MINOR and Systems #1 rates them BLOCKING.** I side with Systems on severity. My reason is the Golem/Staff contradiction above, which is a second, independent cost on top of calibration drift.

### Feasibility Lead

**CONNECTION — Feasibility #5 found my #4's antagonist gaps independently, from the asset side.** Feasibility notes that the level-20 mid-boss is not among the asset count's "2 of its forces", and that the level-40 "Boss level" has no mechanic or interaction rules. Two lenses reaching the same passages raises confidence. The cheapest fix serves both: make the level-20 force the Creep Wraith (no new asset) and make level 40 a "Story card" like 35 and 45. If level 40 does become a real boss mechanic, then my suggestion of a Kancil trick at 40 (my #3) is where its design should come from, and it belongs on Feasibility's cut list.

### REVISIONS

- **#5 (bark names the wrong guardian): MINOR → MAJOR.** Psych #2 shows the bark is the only per-fragment feedback, so from level 51 the sole signal of progress is wrong. It is still a one-line fix and a quick win.
- **#1 (cliffhanger): stays MAJOR, strengthened.** Systems #2 and BA #1 show it is the last content most retained players see before D7 is measured. I now also support the doc's cut-list fallback of ending on the Rat's restoration if 51–60 can't be fixed.
- **#3 (voice rules): stays MAJOR, but the line-count bullets are downgraded.** Counting lines in the level-1 comic and naming beats without Kancil is close to rule-lawyering. The substantive problem is that the trickster identity is dropped after 15, and BA #4 shows that this also weakens the "why switch" pitch. I would accept loosening the counting rule if Kancil gets real tricks in the story and an identity on the board.
- **#4 (antagonists/folklore): split.** The mid-boss and Boss-level items remain MINOR narratively but become a quick win, because Feasibility #5 shows they also carry asset and mechanic cost. The folklore-logic item stays MINOR, with higher priority given BA #2's Lunar New Year audience.
- **#2 (motivation after the mechanic): unchanged at MAJOR.** I concede it does not answer Psych #3's 1–25 hook gap, and it needs Psych #5's rewatch screen to be robust.
